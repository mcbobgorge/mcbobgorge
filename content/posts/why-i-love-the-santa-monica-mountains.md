Growing up in the eastern US, I wasn’t spoiled for choice with massive mountain ranges like the ones out West. Thankfully, I was lucky enough to grow up in New Hampshire, about an hour south of the White Mountains, some of the most impressive and formidable peaks east of the Mississippi. Through high school and college I hiked all 48 of New Hampshire’s 4,000-foot peaks, and all the while I yearned for the big peaks out West. Mount Washington, the tallest peak in the Northeast, is a laughable 6,288 feet. This lack of vert was a primary reason I wanted to move out West when I graduated from college. I lived in Idaho for a stint, but ended up in California, where I’ve been for the past five years.

California is home to the tallest peak in the lower 48, Mount Whitney. Los Angeles, where I live, is surrounded on all sides by mountains and ocean. There are dozens of 10,000-foot peaks that are day hikes from Los Angeles, and the Sierra Nevada is an easy overnight. There are incredible desert peaks to the east, and island peaks to the west. It really is a hiker’s paradise, provided you can avoid traffic. Over the past five years, I’ve stood atop Mount Whitney, Mount Baldy, San Gorgonio, San Jacinto, and dozens more. I’ve scrambled up 13ers in the High Sierra and gazed from the Pacific to the desert in the Santa Ana Mountains. And yet my favorite range is one of the smallest.

The Santa Monica Mountains rise to a paltry 3,100 feet at their highest point, Sandstone Peak. The range is only around 40 miles long by 8 miles wide. You can walk the length of the range in a couple of days, and I have. The mountains aren’t inside the boundaries of a prestigious national park, and while they are close to Los Angeles, they don’t provide a snow-capped, jaw-dropping backdrop like the San Gabriels.

The best part of the Santa Monica Mountains is how alive they feel. There is so much life: 20% of all native plant species in California can be found in this tiny range.[^1] There are mountain lions, raptors, and all kinds of amphibians. Most importantly, there are people. Two million people visit Griffith Park, on the eastern edge of the range, every year. Of course, it is easy to avoid them if you head west into Point Mugu State Park and the Boney Mountain Wilderness. But that proximity to people is important: these mountains are accessible. You can get off the Red Line at the Hollywood/Highland station and be at Runyon Canyon a short 15-minute walk later. There are dozens of trailheads right on the edge of the city’s suburban sprawl, a ten-minute drive from Santa Monica, or less from Brentwood. To me, the best mountains are the ones you actually enjoy. And these are right there, for millions of Angelenos.

![A hammock hanging beside a large oak tree on a dry, grassy hillside](img/why-i-love-the-santa-monica-mountains-3.jpg)

The mountains really come alive after it rains, with all kinds of ephemeral waterfalls and creeks. The grass turns from brown to green, and parts of the range can feel like a jungle. If you’re into geology, there are all kinds of interesting fossils from when the mountains were underwater millions of years ago. If you know where to look, you can also find Chumash pictographs. If none of that interests you, consider that the weather is unbeatable, with year-round hiking thanks to the mountains’ proximity to the Pacific (although it can get a bit hot midday in the summer). That proximity also means you get beautiful ocean views from many of the peaks along the ridge.

One thing that makes the range special to me is its designation: not as a national park or forest, but as a national recreation area. It is a living mountain range, with ranches, houses, and also wilderness. Hiking through these peaks, you stumble across Hollywood filming locations, historic mines, and all kinds of [historical oddities](https://www.nps.gov/samo/planyourvisit/straussranch.htm).

If you’re interested in visiting the Santa Monica Mountains but don’t know where to start, here are some options at various intensity levels.

### Easy: Nike Missile Control Site LA-96C

This is a short hike along a fire road, about two miles round trip, from the Upper Canyonback Trailhead. It is very accessible, only seven minutes west of the 405. The walk takes you to an abandoned missile defense station, with beautiful views from the ocean to Downtown LA.

### Medium: Mishe Mokwa to Tri Peaks

I’ll just link [the AllTrails page](https://www.alltrails.com/trail/us/california/mishe-mokwa-to-tri-peaks-and-sandstone-peak) here. This is a fantastic 7-mile loop in the western part of the range. Sandstone Peak is popular, but this route also loops you around to the less-traveled north side of the peak.

### Hard: The Backbone Trail

The best way to enjoy the range is to hike the 67-mile Backbone Trail, which follows the spine of the range all the way from Point Mugu to Pacific Palisades. This is a tricky route to thru-hike, and will likely require stealth camping and water caching. I did it a few years ago, and it really opened my eyes to the beauty of this range.
